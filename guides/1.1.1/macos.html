MUGIC MOTION — macOS Installation

Version 1.1.1 · macOS 11 Big Sur or later · Intel and Apple Silicon (universal)

Download MUGIC-MOTION-1.1.1-universal.pkg and double-click it.

The installer puts all three formats where your software expects them:

Format Installed to Used by
AU (Audio Unit) /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components Logic Pro, GarageBand, Live
VST3 /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3 Ableton Live, Reaper, Cubase, Bitwig
App (standalone) /Applications no DAW needed

It will ask for your admin password. That's expected — Logic and GarageBand only scan the system plug-in folders, not your home folder, so the plug-ins have to be installed system-wide.

The installer is signed and notarized by Apple (KIMARI, LLC), so it should open without a Gatekeeper warning. If macOS ever does complain, right-click the .pkg → Open → Open.

After installing

Logic Pro / GarageBand — restart the app. Logic validates new Audio Units on launch; MUGIC MOTION appears under Audio FX → Audio Units → KIMARI. If it doesn't show up, run /Applications/Utilities/Audio MIDI Setup or reset Logic's AU cache:

rm -rf ~/Library/Caches/AudioUnitCache

then relaunch Logic.

Ableton Live — Preferences → Plug-Ins → turn on Use VST3 Plug-In System Folders, then Rescan.

Reaper / Cubase / Bitwig — rescan plug-ins in preferences.

Standalone — just open MUGIC MOTION Plugin from Applications.

Connecting your MUGIC device

  1. Power on the MUGIC; put it on the same Wi-Fi network as the Mac.
  2. In the top-left panel check Port (default 4000) and Prefix (default /mugicdata) — these must match what the device sends.
  3. The YAW / ROLL / PITCH readouts start moving when data arrives.

Nothing moving? Usually: different network, another app already holds port 4000, or the Prefix doesn't match the device. Quick test — clear the Prefix field; an empty prefix accepts any address.

The first launch may ask for Local Network permission. Allow it — the device streams over UDP and denying it means no data. To fix later: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network.

Calibrating (changed in 1.0.1)

Click Calibrate Motion, choose an axis, and keep moving that axis back and forth through its full range for the whole countdown. The app records the minimum and maximum you reach and uses that span as the range.

In 1.0.0 you had to hold two separate poses, which was slow and easy to get wrong. From 1.0.1 it's a single sweep per axis — just keep moving until the countdown ends. Each axis is calibrated independently; you don't have to do all three.

Sending MIDI

Choose a MIDI param per MUGIC parameter, set the MIDI MODE (Continuous tracks motion across the full range; Switch sends on/off as you cross THRESH), then click ASSIGN. The button then reads LEARN — click it and the plug-in streams only that one parameter, so your DAW's MIDI-learn latches onto the right control instead of whichever axis moved first.

Set a param's MIDI target to None to mute just that one. The global MIDI ON/OFF button mutes everything.

Controlling the plug-in from your DAW (new in 1.1.0)

The plug-in's buttons are now also parameters your DAW can see, so you can work them from a MIDI controller or an automation lane instead of reaching for the plug-in window mid-performance. The one most people want: put Reset Orientation on a footswitch or a pad and re-centre without touching the Mac.

What's available, for each MUGIC (M1...M12), plus an All version that hits every connected device at once:

Parameter What it does
Reset Orientation Re-centres the device — wherever it's pointing now becomes "straight ahead"
Reset Wrap Clears built-up yaw wrap-around
Reset Range Cal Clears the sweep calibration for that device
MIDI Out Turns that one device's MIDI output on/off — a per-device mute

The three resets are momentary: they fire once when the button goes down, so a controller button that sends on-press / off-release does exactly the right thing, and holding it down won't keep re-firing. MIDI Out is an ordinary on/off switch and stays in step with the MIDI ON/OFF button in the plug-in window, whichever one you change.

Logic Pro — with MUGIC MOTION selected, use Controller Assignments → Learn Mode (or a Smart Control), then press the button on your controller.

Ableton Live — click the plug-in's ⚙/Configure button so the parameters are listed, press Cmd+M for MIDI Map Mode, click the parameter, then press your controller button.

Reaper / Cubase / Bitwig — use the host's usual "learn" on the parameter, or draw it into an automation lane and put a short blip where you want the reset.

Note that the motion values themselves (yaw, pitch, roll, energy, jolt) are not in this list, and that's deliberate — a plug-in parameter is something the DAW sends into the plug-in. Motion still comes out as MIDI, exactly as before; see Sending MIDI above.

Known limitations in this build

  • Yaw left/right may be reversed depending on how the sensor is oriented on your hand or instrument. Pitch and roll are unaffected.
  • Some labels in the left column can be clipped on smaller displays — drag the window larger.
