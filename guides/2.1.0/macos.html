MUGIC MOTION — macOS Installation

Version 2.1.0 · macOS 11 Big Sur or later · Intel and Apple Silicon (universal)

Download MUGIC-MOTION-2.1.0-universal.pkg and double-click it.

The installer puts all three formats where your software expects them:

Format Installed to Used by
AU (Audio Unit) /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components Logic Pro, GarageBand, Live
VST3 /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3 Ableton Live, Reaper, Cubase, Bitwig
App (standalone) /Applications no DAW needed

It will ask for your admin password. That's expected — Logic and GarageBand only scan the system plug-in folders, not your home folder, so the plug-ins have to be installed system-wide.

The installer is signed and notarized by Apple (KIMARI, LLC), so it should open without a Gatekeeper warning. If macOS ever does complain, right-click the .pkg → Open → Open.

After installing

MUGIC MOTION is an instrument, not an audio effect. Look for it wherever your DAW lists synths / instruments — never under Audio FX or audio effects.

Logic Pro / GarageBand — restart the app; Logic validates new Audio Units on launch. Create a Software Instrument track, click the instrument slot, and choose AU Instruments → MUGIC MOTION → MUGIC MOTION Plugin. If it doesn't show up, reset Logic's AU cache:

rm -rf ~/Library/Caches/AudioUnitCache

then relaunch Logic.

Ableton Live — Preferences → Plug-Ins → turn on Use VST3 Plug-In System Folders, then Rescan. It appears under Instruments → MUGIC MOTION Plugin; drag it onto a MIDI track.

Reaper / Cubase / Bitwig — rescan plug-ins in preferences, then look under instruments (VSTi), not audio effects.

Standalone — just open MUGIC MOTION Plugin from Applications.

Connecting your MUGIC device

  1. Power on the MUGIC; put it on the same Wi-Fi network as the Mac.
  2. On the Device tab check UDP port (default 4000) and OSC prefix (default /mugicdata) — these must match what the device sends.
  3. The dot next to the device's name turns green when data arrives.

Nothing moving? Usually: different network, another app already holds port 4000, or the Prefix doesn't match the device. Quick test — clear the Prefix field; an empty prefix accepts any address.

The first launch may ask for Local Network permission. Allow it — the device streams over UDP and denying it means no data. To fix later: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network.

Calibrating

The plug-in opens on the Calibrate tab. Hold MUGIC in your resting position and click Reset Orientation. Then click Calibrate YAW (or PITCH, ROLL) and keep moving that axis back and forth through its full range until the countdown ends. The furthest points you reach become its range: 0 and 127 on MIDI. The orange marks on each axis show the calibrated range. Each axis is calibrated on its own, and Calibration length (5 s by default) sets how long each one lasts.

Speed, Energy and Jolt calibrate from their settings on the Map tab (see below).

Sending MIDI

The Map tab has one row per thing MUGIC measures: YAW, PITCH and ROLL angles, ENERGY and JOLT, and — under the arrow next to each axis — that axis's speed and steadiness (new in 2.0). In Sends as, type a CC number or pick a named controller or pitch bend; none mutes that row. Continuous tracks the motion; Switch is on while above "Switch at" and off below; Toggle flips on/off each time the motion rises past it. Every change takes effect immediately.

A 14 button sits next to any CC from 0 to 31. Turn it on to send that CC at high resolution — 16,384 steps instead of 128 — as the standard 14-bit pair (CC 14 + CC 46, CC 1 + CC 33, …); or just type a + after the number, e.g. 14+. Use it only when the synth or DAW receiving it understands 14-bit CC (set its assignment to 14-bit); anything else sees two unrelated controllers.

The sliders button on a row opens its settings: its MIDI Min / Max and channel, and how it is measured. Speed has Calibrate speed (move as fast as you play); Energy has Rest and Play hard; Jolt shows its live signal against the threshold and can Suggest threshold.

Click LEARN on a row and the plug-in streams only that one, so your DAW's MIDI-learn latches onto the right control instead of whichever axis moved first.

MIDI ON/OFF (top right) mutes the selected device; each device has its own.

Getting the MIDI into Ableton Live or Logic Pro

Out decides where MUGIC's MIDI goes, and the right choice depends on what you want the motion to do.

To MIDI-learn a control (LEARN button) — use a MIDI port, not DAW (Host). Both Ableton's MIDI Map mode and Logic's Learn Mode only listen to MIDI ports; they never see MIDI that comes out of a plug-in, so with DAW (Host) the LEARN button has nothing to latch onto.

  1. Open Audio MIDI Setup → Window → Show MIDI Studio, double-click IAC Driver and tick Device is online (one time only).
  2. In MUGIC MOTION, set Out to IAC Driver Bus 1.
  3. Ableton Live: Settings → Link, Tempo & MIDI, and turn on Remote for the IAC Driver (Bus 1) input. Then Cmd+M, click the control, click LEARN in MUGIC MOTION and move. Logic Pro: Logic Pro → Control Surfaces → Learn Assignment, click the control, click LEARN and move.

To play an instrument on another track:

  • Ableton Live — DAW (Host) works. On the instrument's track set MIDI From to the MUGIC MOTION track (second menu: MUGIC MOTION) and Monitor to In.
  • Logic Pro — Logic ignores MIDI that an instrument plug-in sends out, so DAW (Host) can't reach another track. Use the IAC bus as above; any track record-armed or selected will receive it.

If something goes wrong

Click Clear log on the Device tab (or in the ? help window), make the problem happen again, then click Show log file (or open ~/Library/Logs/MUGIC MOTION/mugic.log in Finder) and send that file with your report. It's plain text: open it in TextEdit and delete anything you'd rather not share first. It only holds the plug-in's own actions, and keeps just the most recent part.

Controlling the plug-in from your DAW (new in 1.1.0)

The plug-in's buttons are now also parameters your DAW can see, so you can work them from a MIDI controller or an automation lane instead of reaching for the plug-in window mid-performance. The one most people want: put Reset Orientation on a footswitch or a pad and re-centre without touching the Mac.

What's available, for each MUGIC (M1...M12), plus an All version that hits every connected device at once:

Parameter What it does
Reset Orientation Re-centres the device — wherever it's pointing now becomes "straight ahead"
Reset Wrap Clears built-up yaw wrap-around
Reset Range Cal Clears the sweep calibration for that device
MIDI Out Turns that one device's MIDI output on/off — a per-device mute

The three resets are momentary: they fire once when the button goes down, so a controller button that sends on-press / off-release does exactly the right thing, and holding it down won't keep re-firing. MIDI Out is an ordinary on/off switch and stays in step with the MIDI ON/OFF button in the plug-in window, whichever one you change.

Logic Pro — with MUGIC MOTION selected, use Controller Assignments → Learn Mode (or a Smart Control), then press the button on your controller.

Ableton Live — click the plug-in's ⚙/Configure button so the parameters are listed, press Cmd+M for MIDI Map Mode, click the parameter, then press your controller button.

Reaper / Cubase / Bitwig — use the host's usual "learn" on the parameter, or draw it into an automation lane and put a short blip where you want the reset.

Note that the motion values themselves (yaw, pitch, roll, energy, jolt) are not in this list, and that's deliberate — a plug-in parameter is something the DAW sends into the plug-in. Motion still comes out as MIDI, exactly as before; see Sending MIDI above.

Known limitations in this build

  • Yaw left/right may be reversed depending on how the sensor is oriented on your hand or instrument. Pitch and roll are unaffected.
